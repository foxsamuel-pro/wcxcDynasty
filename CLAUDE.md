# WCXC Poll Tracker

Power-rankings voting site for WCXC Dynasty, a 12-team superflex, TE-premium dynasty league on Sleeper. Live at wcxcdynasty.site.

## Stack
- `index.html`: the whole site in one file (HTML, CSS, JS). No build step.
- Ballots live in the Cloudflare D1 database `wcxc` (tables in `schema.sql`), reached only through the site's Worker (`server/worker.mjs`, rules in `server/ballots.mjs`):
  - `ballots` (season, week, voter = Sleeper roster_id, ranking best-first), publicly readable through `GET /api/ballots`
  - `team_passwords` (one row per team, PBKDF2-hashed, never served)
  - `POST /api/ballot`, the only write path. It checks or sets the team's password, validates the ballot and enforces the voting window.
  - Pick 'em was removed from the site entirely; don't bring it back piecemeal.
- Auth model: per-team password, like the old PIN but a normal password field (no numeric-only restriction). A team's first ballot sets its password; every ballot after needs the same one. Reading is open to everyone; only casting a ballot is gated, and only for that one team.
  - No push channel: the page re-reads ballots after a vote, on a timer and when the browser tab comes back into focus.
- **The casino, article comments and Supabase are gone** (the Supabase project was deleted in November 2026). Don't bring them back piecemeal.
- Sleeper API (no auth) for teams, records, avatars, divisions, rosters and the schedule. League ID: 1312128506452283392
- **This league plays two results per week** (`league_average_match`): head-to-head *and* the league median, so the regular season is 28 results, not 14. Verified against week 1 — H2H+median reproduces all 12 Sleeper records. Any standings/simulation work must account for both.
- **Records come straight from Sleeper's roster totals** (commissioner's call, Oct 2026): the page calls `catchUpRecords({addWeeks:false})`, which only notes how many weeks those totals cover, so a finished week shows once Sleeper posts it. The add-a-week path still exists and is tested; it adds a week only after every NFL game is complete, including head-to-head and median results. Anchor the simulation's first open week on the displayed records' completed-week count, not `state.week`, so every week is banked or simulated exactly once. Efficiency uses `pfOff` / `ppOff` for matching official weeks. Offline regressions: `node --test tests/catchup.test.js`.
- Playoff odds use 10,000 player-level simulated seasons, generated daily into `odds.json` by `scripts/odds/build.mjs`. The browser reads the dated snapshot, independently of the Analysis schedule cache. The Poll table also loads this snapshot and labels it as the latest forecast even on historical polls. Full assumptions, data sources, and validation: `docs/playoff-odds.md`.
- **Sleeper's `winners_bracket` is a PROJECTION until the regular season is over** — it is filled in from the day's standings all season. Model 2 took its first-round pairings as real, so a team could reach the final twice (roster 1: 82% final, 49% title in Week 5). Model 3 reads the bracket only when `firstOpen > lastRegular` (tested). The page accepts model 2 or 3. Anything else that reads the bracket must check the season is scored first.
- Player forecast ranks drive a weighted lottery over historical position-specific PPG profiles under league scoring. Each assigned baseline PPG lasts a whole simulated season. Weekly mean and SD are multiplied by that week's projection divided by the player's average positive projection in active weeks, so playoff matchups matter. Weekly normal variation, exact legal lineups selected before scores, NFL byes, and randomized absences apply through the actual playoff weeks. Team results bank standings; they never adjust player talent. Polls are never an input.
- Three division winners qualify; the top two get byes. Three wildcards plus the remaining winner seed on record/PF/higher PA. Preserve H2H and median ties; playoff ties favor the higher seed. No playoff median games.
- Exclude taxi from lineups, retain IR for weeks with positive projections, and lock completed player scores in their actual lineup slots. Fail closed on incomplete data or NFL games in progress. Keep the last good odds file and show its date; never silently fall back to a different model.
- `.github/workflows/odds.yml` updates each morning around 6:17 AM Eastern with retry slots and no Claude dependency. `--daily` guards against duplicate daily publications. Historical cache keys include scoring, season, and model version; increment model version when changing fitting assumptions. Do not restore the obsolete team-aggregate projection model.
- Offline regressions: `node --test tests/odds*.test.mjs tests/trades.test.mjs tests/catchup.test.js` (the same set `odds.yml` runs).
- **Trade archive**: `trades.json`, built by `scripts/trades/build.mjs`, rebuilt daily by the odds workflow. Every trade since 2023 with what each side has scored *since*. Sleeper records a traded pick only as (season, round, original owner) — resolve it via the draft's `slot_to_roster_id` (original owner → slot, slot + round → the selection made). A season can hold several drafts (2023 had a 25-round startup in February and a 5-round rookie draft in May), so pick whichever was still upcoming when the trade happened.
- **Three measures, deliberately not blended.** `started` = points scored *while actually in that roster's starting lineup* (take WHO started from matchups but the points from the same weekly stats table the totals use, or the two scales diverge). `points` = everything scored. `value` = today's dynasty market from FantasyCalc, fetched at build time in CI so the CSP is untouched, matched on `sleeperId` — no name matching, which is what made the old KTC attempt unreliable. **The feed accepts only dynasty/numQbs/numTeams/ppr.** This league is half PPR (`ppr=0.5`, not 1) and it is *also* TE premium with points per first down, neither of which can be requested — `tePremium`/`teBonus`/`ppfd` are accepted and silently ignored (byte-identical responses). So `positionalScalars()` corrects each position by how far league scoring lifts it relative to the rest, measured over every completed season: TE ~1.21, WR ~0.94, RB ~0.97, QB ~1.01. Recomputes itself if scoring changes; don't hardcode it. Value is the only measure that can price the ~75 unused picks; on points alone a rebuild scores zero forever. They disagree on the winner in over a third of trades, which is the point — don't collapse them into one score with invented weights. **Market value is the headline and the sort**: a trade is a swap of assets, so what those assets are worth is the verdict, and it is the only measure that can price an unused pick.
- **The raw points-scored total was removed** (Oct 2026). It cannot tell a bench 20 from a starting 20 and scores a rebuild at zero forever. `started` stays as "In lineup"; `points` is still computed and stored but is not a column.
- **`swing` = value when traded vs value now** ("Bought well"), from a *second* feed. FantasyCalc publishes no history, so `scripts/trades/history.mjs` reads DynastyProcess's `files/values.csv` out of its public git repo — 364 weekly commits back to 2019-02-13, fetched per-sha and cached immutably, column `value_2qb`. **The two feeds price on scales that differ ~5x on picks** (a 2027 2nd is 311 there, 1,551 at FantasyCalc), so both ends of a swing come from DynastyProcess alone and the result is only ever shown as a *change* — never a total beside a FantasyCalc price. Matching is by name (no `sleeperId` in that feed); strip generational suffixes or match rate drops ~7 points. Coverage is ~70% of assets; an asset priced at only one end is excluded, never guessed.
  - A pick's "then" is the **pick's** price and its "now" is the **player's** — two different assets on purpose, which is the question. No tier label on the "then" side: today's draft order isn't the one that stood then.
  - Deliberately **not** filtered by whether the team still holds the asset, unlike `value`. Flipping it on later is a separate decision, judged in its own row. Two trades measuring the same player over different windows is two measurements, not double-counted production, so the direct-return rule still holds.
  - A trade dated after `market.historyThrough` gets **no** swing — comparing a snapshot against itself is +0% for everything, which is not a finding.
- **Every measure is a net of got against gave, so each must sum to exactly zero across the league** (asserted in tests). Do **not** compute "gave" as the other sides' hauls: in the two 3-way trades that charges a team for assets it never owned and left the league 18,823 points of value richer than it started. `gaveValue`/`gaveStarted`/`gave` are attributed at build time from Sleeper's own record of the sender — `drops` for a player, `previous_owner_id` for a pick.
- **`movedOn` needs evidence, not presence.** "Traded on" is a claim about a specific team, so it means *a later trade in which THAT team sent THAT pick* — match on `previous_owner_id`. Being merely present in a later trade is not proof: wherever the archive loses a link in a chain, the team still holding the pick gets told it traded it away. This has been wrong twice, in opposite directions — comparing trade ids alone ignored time (28 picks marked as moved on by *earlier* trades), then taking every later trade over-claimed (17 picks). Both also stripped the pick's market value from its real owner, because `stillOurs` derives from this. Tests assert every claim against the sender record.
- **A pick's identity must include which draft it is for**, not just season + round + original owner. 2023 ran two drafts, so "Shake and Bake's 2023 1st" is *two* picks — one became Lamar Jackson, the other Bijan Robinson. Merging them produced a chain where a team appeared to send one pick twice without receiving it back. `identity()` appends the resolved draft id, and each pick row carries `draft` as provenance.
- One chain is genuinely broken in Sleeper's own data: roster 1 sends its 2027 3rd twice with no trade returning it, and 2027 has one draft. That's a source gap, not a merge — don't try to "fix" it. With evidence required, the pick just stays with whoever last received it.
- A player can finish on **negative** points (turnovers), so `started <= points` is not a universal invariant — Jalen Milroe sits at −1.7 having never started.
- **Grading stops at the direct return.** A side scores its players received plus the players drafted with its picks received. A pick traded on again counts toward the *later* deal and carries a `movedOn` trail instead — otherwise two trades claim the same production and a long chain credits one ancient deal with half a roster. Don't "improve" this into full lineage scoring.
- **That rule is about POINTS only. Market value never depends on who holds the asset now.** What a trade handed over is what it handed over; selling it on afterwards is a separate transaction, and the later trade debits it there. Pricing only current holdings made a team that flipped a pick look as though it had received nothing, and because the same flag suppressed the price, a falsely-flagged pick silently stripped value from its real owner. This does **not** double-count: one side's haul is the other's loss at the identical price, so `value`, `started` and `swing` each sum to exactly zero across the twelve teams (asserted). `kept`/`movedOn` are labels now, nothing more.
- Franchise identity is the roster id (stable here), but `sameManager` flags trades made by a predecessor — roster 9 changed hands. Three trades have only one side in Sleeper's data; they're kept and flagged `oneSided`, not dropped. Tests: `node --test tests/trades.test.mjs tests/trade-history.test.mjs`.
- **Stats tab** (`renderStats` and the `/* ---------- stats` block, after `render()`): league history since 2023. `scripts/stats/build.mjs` writes `stats.json` (each season raw from Sleeper: weeks as `[roster, matchup, points]`, brackets as-is, Sleeper's own W-L-T per roster); `odds.yml` rebuilds it daily and it has no timestamp so it only commits on a change. The page derives everything from two lists, `rows` (one team-week) and `games` (one head-to-head result; playoff games are decided winners-bracket matches with the round's summed points and Sleeper's winner), and refreshes the current season live from Sleeper. Records count the median like Sleeper; `tests/stats.test.mjs` proves every team's W-L-T matches Sleeper's totals in every season. 2023-24 playoffs were two weeks a round, so those games stay out of single-game lists. Names are today's (Sleeper keeps no history); `prev` marks a season under a previous manager. Keep this block out of the `const pct =` .. `function render(){` range: `tests/odds-ui.test.mjs` evaluates that slice without `store`.
- Hosting: a Cloudflare Worker with static assets (`wrangler.jsonc`), deployed by Workers Builds on every push to `main`. Only `/api/*` runs Worker code. Custom domain wcxcdynasty.site, DNS on Cloudflare. `netlify.toml` is kept only as a fallback and is inert on Cloudflare.
- `_headers` sets the security headers and is read by both Cloudflare Pages and Netlify — it's the one place headers are defined. It includes a CSP that allowlists every host the page talks to: `sleepercdn.com` for team avatars and `api.sleeper.app` for league data. Adding a CDN, analytics script or font host means updating it or the browser blocks the request silently.
- Keep Cloudflare **Rocket Loader off** for this site — it rewrites script tags and can break the inline bootstrap.

## Conventions
- News publishing rules and setup live in `docs/news-publishing.md`. The GitHub workflow prompts Claude Code using `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` (subscription sign-in), never a direct AI API key. Wednesday 5 PM ET: poll at 8 votes, otherwise satire. Thursday 5 PM: poll if not already published, otherwise satire. TNF/SNF/MNF get pregame and postgame; Sunday also gets noon and 4 PM previews, and its SNF recap covers the whole day. Only non-game days have a one-article daily cap, and that cap applies to the daily slot alone. Completed trades and injuries to players in a starting lineup are a separate `moves` edition that runs any day, deduped by `txIds`/`injIds` on published articles and limited to events from the last 48h. Tests: `node --test tests/news-*.test.mjs`.
- Keep it a single static `index.html` unless there's a real reason to split it.
- Voting window: **Tue 12:00 AM ET → Thu 8:00 PM ET**. `votingOpen() = windowOpen() && S.week === ballotWeek()`. Other weeks/times render `renderClosed()` and stay fully readable. A 30s `tick()` opens and closes the form live, no reload. The RPC still accepts any week on purpose, as a commissioner backfill path.
- `pollWeekFor()` = the week whose games are being played, counting Tuesday-to-Tuesday from `season_start_date` — **not** Sleeper's `state.week`, which doesn't advance at a reliable time. `ballotWeek()` = the week a ballot is FOR: the same thing while the window is open, but the *next* week once it shuts, because ballots cast Tuesday are the rankings going into that week. Don't conflate them — the countdown must name `ballotWeek()`.
- All time math is `America/New_York` and DST-safe (whole-day counters, not ms arithmetic).
- The week dropdown only lists weeks that have happened (`latestWeek()` = max of the played week, the voting week, and any week with ballots). Never offer an empty Week 12 in September.
- Past weeks keep everything: ballots are `(season, week, voter)` and `loadBallots()` pulls the whole season, so Poll/Grid/Spread/Season/Voters all work retroactively.
- Responsive in one stylesheet, no separate mobile page. Breakpoints: **860px** (the rail becomes a bottom tab bar), **760px** (the main mobile pass) and **380px** (small phones). Wide stat tables get `class="cards"` plus `data-l` on each `td.stat` so they restack as cards; wide matrix tables get `class="matrix"` to pin the first column. The shell is one `.shell` grid: a sticky `.rail` beside the content on a desktop, and on a phone that rail moves to the bottom while `.topbar` carries the brand and week picker.
- **Every lazy loader needs a terminal flag.** `loadTrades` had none: a failed fetch left `TRADES` null and cleared `tradesLoading`, so `renderTrades` called it straight back — ~200 requests in three seconds for as long as the tab stayed open. `loadOdds` latches `oddsLoaded` and `loadNews` latches `NEWS = []`; `loadTrades` now latches `tradesLoaded` and says the archive failed instead of claiming to still be loading.
- Local preview: VS Code Live Server (auto-reload), or `npx serve .`. Never push just to look at a change.
- **The News archive filters by week and by category** (`S.newsWeek`, `S.newsKind`). Each filter's counts are taken with the other applied, so a number is how many stories picking it gives; the week picker appears once there is more than one week to choose; its kicker reads "League wire" (it used to say the site's poll week, which contradicted a week picked here). Tests: `tests/news-filter.test.mjs`.
- **Five sections, not ten tabs**: Poll / Vote / News / Outlook / Trades, in `TABS`. Ten did not fit the bottom of a phone, and four were views of the same thing. The ballot grid, the season chart and the voter reports are now views *inside* Poll (`S.pv`, listed in `VIEWS`, drawn by `viewSeg()` under the heading); the playoff forecast and the poll-vs-results analysis are both **Outlook**, which renders `renderOdds(true)` then `renderAnalysis(true)` into one page. `MOVED` maps every retired tab name to where it went, and `goTo()` translates it, so a stored tab or an old `data-go` link still lands somewhere sensible — add to `MOVED` rather than breaking a link. The vote distribution heat table lives at the bottom of the Ballot grid view (`distPanel()`), not on a tab of its own — it answers the question the grid raises.
- **One dark palette**, no light variant. Surfaces step `--bg` → `--panel` → `--row` → `--row-2`; type is Barlow Condensed (`--head`, for headings and numbers) over Figtree.
- **The accent is blue (`--acc`) everywhere.** Green on this site *means* something — a team moving up, an open voting window — so it cannot also mean "this is a button". Ratios are measured, not eyeballed: `--acc-ink` on `--acc` is 6.43:1, `--acc` on a panel 5.99:1, `--acc` on `--acc-bg` 4.73:1.
- The analysis half of Outlook is **metric × scope × view**, not a pile of charts — that's what keeps it from cluttering the site. Adding a metric means one entry in `METRICS` plus a case in `metricValues()`; don't add a new tab for it. Gap = stat rank − poll rank and must always sum to zero across teams (asserted in tests).
- Gap bars use the diverging pair `--over` / `--under`, validated on this surface (ΔE 43.1). Scatter is one hue + direct labels. Never introduce 12 categorical colours.
- The Season chart uses the emphasis form (all 12 teams gray, hovered/clicked one in blue) — 12 categorical hues can't stay distinguishable. Colors are `--em` / `--ctx`, measured at ΔE 34.6 against a floor of 15; see README before changing them.
- **Satire is built around a player reacting to his fantasy team** — a trade demand, a grievance about the record, the lineup, his teammates or the manager. Invent quotes, attitude and absurd demands freely and commit to the bit; don't hedge, wink or append disclaimers. The Satire badge is the only label. Keep the league numbers real — the joke works because the record and projections are accurate. **One limit**, about real people rather than tone: no invented real-world wrongdoing or health claims for a named player (crimes, drugs, bigotry, sexual content, injuries, medical conditions). Grumpy, vain and deluded are fine.
- **Satire should let things happen, not just quote people.** Breaking into a rival's facility, a reassigned locker, an unauthorised vote, paperwork, a parallel practice in a car park — petty crime, bureaucracy and property disputes inside the league are all in range and beat another quote about effort. The invention is in the events; the record and scores stay real.
- **Most teams keep a retired player on the roster as coaching staff**, and they are the best characters available. Declared in `COACHES` in `index.html` as `"Name, role"` (semicolons for several), read by `siteConfig()` and surfaced as each team's `staff`. **Not derivable** — Nick Chubb played 15 games in 2025 and is Beasty Boys' head coach, while Tyreek Hill is equally teamless and is just a stashed player. **Roles vary**: Tyler Lockett is Chinese Sweatshop's *player development* coach. Use the role exactly; never promote, reassign, invent a coach for a team with no entry, or call an active player staff.
- **Never name a lineup slot.** Flex vs receiver, superflex vs quarterback is bookkeeping — no manager sees a player that way and it never makes a sentence land. Sleeper's `starters` array is ordered by slot, which is how the writer learned to say "a zero in the flex spot"; the prompt no longer recites the slot order. Eligibility still matters when a joke depends on who *could* start (four spots take a TE, two QBs can start) — know the rule, never recite it.
- **Never predict an empty lineup spot.** `max_subs` is 3, so a doubtful or ruled-out starter is usually replaced before kickoff; "they'll take a zero there" has been wrong in print. After the games it's a fact and fair to write. Tests: `node --test tests/news-voice.test.mjs`.
- **Publishing nothing is a valid outcome.** The gameless-day satire slot requires something to have happened — a trade inside 72h, or games the day before to react to. It used to fire on any quiet day purely because the day existed, which produced articles with no hook that nobody wanted to read. `moves.injuries` is *currently* injured, not newly, so it is never a hook.
- **Don't force an edition just because a slot is due.** An injury is only a story if the player matters: `loadSnapshot` gates on Sleeper's `search_rank` (top 75). Being in a `starters` array proves nothing — managers leave ruled-out reserves in lineups they haven't fixed. Jonah Coleman, a rookie third on his depth chart projected for zero, once triggered a whole article. Genuine stories sit near the top of that rank (Nico Collins 23, Jayden Daniels 20); filler doesn't (Coleman 118). If the day's news is thin, satire is the better edition.
- **Never build a story on site mechanics.** Who has voted, ballots outstanding, voting deadlines and engagement are not news. A poll *result* is league news; poll *turnout* is not. A piece premised on "three teams haven't voted" was rejected outright.
- **Articles name the TEAM, not the person** — "REFUGEES are 4-0", never "Sam is 4-0". A manager first name is only for something a person did (a trade, a ballot). Second reference uses `SHORT_NAMES` verbatim: Parkers Dead Sons is "Dead Sons", Latina Lip-Pickers is "Lip-Pickers". Never shorten a name by guesswork.
- News facts carry a **computed** `record`/`pointsFor`/`pointsAgainst` (H2H + median for every completed week). `officialRecord` is Sleeper's lagging copy — never quote it in an article.
- Draft picks in a trade carry provenance and value: `roster_id` on a Sleeper pick is its ORIGINAL owner, so a pick reads "2027 3rd via Chinese Sweatshop". Order is by season **max points** (ppts, ascending — worst roster picks first), so a bad team's pick is the valuable one. Only the NEXT draft is projectable (this season sets it); a 2028 slot depends on an unplayed season and must show none. Never explain the ordering rule in an article.
- **"vs" on an article means those two teams played each other.** It is a claim, not decoration. `artFigure()` only renders it when `opposedPair()` finds a real Sleeper pairing in the article's `box` — two teams in a story isn't enough, since a trade has two teams and they're partners, not opponents. Everything else gets the neutral strip.
- A trade article ends with a `deals` block built from the transaction record, not "Players to watch". It is generated in `assembleArticle`, never written, so it cannot be misreported.
- **Managers are named by real first name, never their Sleeper handle.** `MANAGERS` (roster_id -> name) overrides `display_name` when building `TEAMS`. Nobody in the league calls Chip "Chipster04". Any news prompt gets real names too.
- Matchup win probability uses **position-aware** spread (QB .55, RB .75, WR .85, TE .80 of projection). A flat factor was wrong — it gave 13% where Sleeper said 16%; position-aware reproduces Sleeper exactly. Remaining output must account for whether a player's NFL game is over (`/scores/nfl/regular/{season}/{week}` has `status`), or underperformers get credited points they can never earn.
- Poll scoring: 12 points for 1st down to 1 point for 12th. Ties broken by first-place votes, then points for.
- Test locally with `npx serve .` or `python3 -m http.server` for the static page; `npx wrangler dev` also runs `/api` against a local D1 (`npx wrangler d1 execute wcxc --local --file schema.sql` once).
