# Daily playoff odds

The site reads `odds.json`, generated by `node scripts/odds/build.mjs`. Each
snapshot contains **1,000 complete simulated seasons**, through the championship.
The poll is never an input. Completed team results affect standings, not the
forward-looking player rankings.

## Inputs and historical profiles

Sleeper supplies the current league settings, rosters, remaining fantasy matchups,
NFL schedule, weekly player projections, and raw player statistics. The three
completed NFL seasons preceding the league season supply historical profiles
(2023–2025 for 2026). Raw stats are rescored using every key in the current league's
scoring settings, including first downs and TE bonuses. Historical bonus fields
already contain the appropriate counts and are not added twice.

Within each historical season and position, players are ranked by Sleeper's archived
season projection under this scoring system. Cohort limits are QB48, RB100, WR140,
and TE80. Profiles require four games with offensive snaps; those games include
low and negative scores. Each profile contains an active-game PPG and a weekly
standard deviation. Variances are shrunk toward the position's pooled variance
using eight prior degrees of freedom, to reduce instability in small samples.

Current players are ranked within position by their average positive remaining-week
projection. The lottery includes projected free agents, so the talent distribution
does not depend on fantasy ownership. A rostered player with no positive future
projection is unavailable, unless preserving an already locked score. Current
rosters are otherwise held fixed; taxi players cannot start, while IR players can
return when their weekly forecasts become positive.

## Weighted season lottery

For each simulated season and position:

1. Choose one of the three historical seasons uniformly.
2. Give current positional rank `r` the lottery weight `r^(-alpha)`.
3. Generate a weighted permutation using independent exponential arrival times:
   `-log(U) / weight`. Earlier arrivals receive stronger PPG profiles.
4. Map the permutation's ranks to evenly spaced empirical quantiles of that
   historical cohort's PPG distribution, carrying the matched weekly deviation.
   Quantile mapping accommodates different historical and current pool sizes;
   more than one player can receive the same empirical profile.
5. Keep each assigned PPG and deviation fixed for the entire simulated season,
   including the playoffs.

This is a [Plackett–Luce ranking distribution](https://hturner.github.io/PlackettLuce/articles/Overview.html).
For each position, `alpha` minimizes historical ranking negative log likelihood
over a grid from 0.25 to 6 in steps of 0.25. High-ranked players are favored, but
no forecast rank guarantees a particular outcome. The season-wide talent draw
creates uncertainty that persists across weeks instead of disappearing when
weekly noise averages out.

## Weekly lineups and scores

NFL schedule data determines byes. Zero weekly projections also make a player
unavailable that week. Additional absences are sampled at position-specific rates
estimated from missed games among historical established players: at least eight
appearances and forecast rank within QB24/RB48/WR72/TE24. The rate is
`(missed games + 1) / (17 × player-seasons + 10)`, with one NFL bye excluded.
This is a modestly regularized **absence proxy**, not a medical injury model:
the feed cannot separate injuries, benching, and other reasons for missing games.
Additional absences are independent by week; known multiweek absences are represented
only to the extent that the weekly projection feed reflects them.

Each fantasy lineup is an exact maximum-weight assignment of available players to
legal starting slots. This handles dual eligibility, ordinary flex, and superflex
without starting anyone twice. Selection uses the assigned expected PPG **before**
drawing that week's random scores. It does not pick the highest realized scores
after the games. A player's weekly score is `Normal(assigned PPG, profile SD)`;
negative fantasy points remain possible, rather than truncating the normal and
artificially raising its mean. Team totals are rounded to cents before results.

Finished NFL games lock the actual starters, their scores, and their lineup slots.
Already-finished bench players cannot be inserted into a lineup. A snapshot is
deferred while any NFL game is in progress; the morning schedule normally avoids
this situation. All completed regular-season weeks are banked exactly once,
including a catch-up when Sleeper's roster totals lag the completed matchup feed.

## Standings and playoffs

The builder validates this league's 12-team, three-division, six-playoff-team,
single-week-round format against Sleeper. A format change fails the update instead
of silently applying the wrong bracket.

- Regular season: head-to-head **and** the league median, with ties preserved.
  Points for and against count once per actual matchup, not twice for the median.
- Standings: winning percentage (ties worth half a win), then higher PF, then
  higher PA. Otherwise unresolved exact ties use a random draw per simulation.
- Three division winners qualify. The best two get byes; three wildcards join the
  third division winner, with seeds 3–6 sorted by the same standings rules.
- Week 15: 3 vs. 6 and 4 vs. 5. Week 16: the bye teams join the survivors, with
  reseeding controlled by the league's `playoff_seed_type`. Week 17: championship.
  The dates are derived from `playoff_week_start`, not projection averages.
- Each playoff week has its own NFL schedule, player availability, optimal lineup,
  and score draws. Completed results in the actual winners bracket are retained.
  Tied playoff games advance the higher seed. No median games occur in playoffs.

Rules: [division qualification and byes](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/2203518-how-do-playoff-teams-get-determined),
[standings and playoff tiebreakers](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4238872-can-i-set-tiebreakers),
[regular-season-only median games](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/3971690-extra-game-each-week-against-league-median).
Commissioner overrides to automatic playoff seeding require reviewing the model;
the forecast assumes the published division/seeding rules.

## Validation and limitations

Run `node --test tests/odds*.test.mjs tests/catchup.test.js`. Tests compare optimal
lineups against exhaustive enumeration, verify locked slots and scores, exercise
weighted lottery behavior, and check division byes, ties, actual playoff weeks,
injured-player returns, and probability conservation. Every production build must
sum to six playoff qualifiers, three division winners, two byes, two finalists,
and one champion before replacing the published snapshot.

The JSON also reports a retrospective ranking check: fit `alpha` using the first
two historical seasons, then compare ranking log loss on the third season against
an equally weighted lottery. Final simulations fit all three seasons. This checks
whether forecast rank adds information; it is **not** an out-of-sample validation
of team playoff probabilities. Sleeper's archived projections are not guaranteed
to be frozen preseason forecasts. A proper prospective calibration study requires
collecting forecasts and outcomes over additional completed seasons.

In the initial 2026 fit, the held-out ranking loss improves over uniform weights
for RB, WR, and TE. QB is slightly worse (2.830 versus 2.826 nats per player),
which is evidence to treat quarterback ranking uncertainty cautiously, not proof
of calibrated quarterback forecasts. The final QB fit uses all three seasons
and a weaker ranking preference than the first-two-season fit (alpha 1.0 vs. 1.5).

Displayed probabilities are successes divided by 1,000. Each includes a
[95% Wilson sampling interval](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm).
Near 50%, sampling uncertainty is approximately ±3.1 percentage points. Zero or
1,000 successes do not establish mathematical elimination or clinching. These
intervals measure Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty only, not model error.

Remaining limitations include conditional independence of weekly player scores,
normal rather than skewed score distributions, ideal expected-point lineup
management, historical cohort survivorship, simplified absence rates, current
player metadata applied to historical positions, and no future trades or waiver
pickups. Forecasts use ranks rather than treating small differences in projected
PPG as precise. The model is an explicit approximation with auditable assumptions,
not a claim of perfectly calibrated probabilities.

## Daily publishing

`.github/workflows/odds.yml` runs independently of news and Claude authentication.
It attempts an update around **6:17 AM Eastern**, with later retry slots. UTC
cron slots cover both standard and daylight time; the builder skips before 6 AM
Eastern and after a successful snapshot on the same Eastern date. GitHub can delay
scheduled jobs. A manual workflow dispatch forces a fresh snapshot.

Historical profiles are cached by season, scoring settings, and model version.
The random seed is derived from the input hash, so identical inputs reproduce the
same simulation instead of changing on each page view. All fetches, calculations,
and validations finish before an atomic replacement of `odds.json`. Failed builds
preserve the last successful file. The page marks forecasts older than 36 hours or
behind the displayed completed records; it does not switch to another model.

To revise the historical-fitting code or its cohort assumptions, increment
`MODEL_VERSION` and the browser's accepted version together, invalidating the
historical cache. No API key or AI subscription is needed for this workflow.
